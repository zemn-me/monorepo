SEEING

01 / Street style

Seeing
Triple

By Kit Zauhar

It feels like everyone in Chinatown has the same style because we’re all just too concerned with looking special. A certain joie de vivre of dressing under a more powerful philosophy is most aptly found, I think, in a group I’ve deemed “The Chinatown Triplets.”

These women, probably in their sixties or seventies (who can appear in a pair as well), are always decked out in matching bright outfits that often involve neon green puffers and orange beanies, their arms linked together dutifully in a way that intensifies their daily strolls into an almost holy practice.

Their devotion to each other, forgoing any and all individuality to appear a united front of collaborative swag, has always struck me as tender and profound, and proves that sometimes a dedication to homogeneity can produce the most inspiring outfit(s) on the block.

02 / A local institution

Henry Wong:
Asian Elvis

Late in the afternoon on Monday September 14 I had the pleasure of speaking with the one and only Henry Wong. Henry has been busy; last year he appeared in Darren Aronofsky’s Caught Stealing, and since then, he has modeled for the likes of H&M, Nike, and Sundae School.

Henry’s stock is way up. When he’s not out being a model and movie star, Henry runs AJ Plumbing Supply, where he’s been working alongside his uncle for over 30 years.

Leaned against a pile of cast iron radiators in AJ Plumbing’s warehouse, he was wearing an Underarmour compression tank, tight blue jeans, decked out in silver jewelry, and sporting his signature blond mullet (Sit down, Bushwick). I knew for certain that I was talking to the most stylish man south of Houston.

“I love music, but I couldn’t sing. I have no talent on the instruments”

I have with much love and admiration always referred to Henry Wong as “Asian Elvis” when I am trying to describe him to neighbors. He does not look like Elvis at all (though he tells me that actually yes, a lot of people call him this), but he does exude a certain rock and roll panache, and when I asked him about his relationship to personal style he brought up how dressing connected him to music.

My somewhat parasocial relationship to Henry began about 5 years ago; I had just become super and a tenant’s bathroom sink drain required replacement parts. I walked in to AJ Plumbing, where Henry helped me patiently. “In this business you’ve got to be patient”.

I did not know the name of the part I needed, I had brought it in fresh off the sink, soaking wet and rusty. Each time I have been back he has been kinder than the last and asked me “Hey, so are you half-Chinese?”

03 / Underfoot

All that
Glitters
is Allenite

By Jacqueline Scheider

Next time you find yourself on the Allen Street Malls between Delancey and Hester Streets, don’t just use the narrow strip to cross the street. Look down at the diamond-shaped pavers and explore an urban gem field of sparkling blue and green crystals.

I call this material ‘Allenite’. Akin to Fordite (the paint agate harvested from the Ford auto factory), this mineral is man-made—but it doesn’t make it any less beautiful.

Made from recycled glass, Allenite follows the longstanding NYC tradition of turning waste into craft. The Lenape people and Dutch colonizers alike had a taste for oysters, the latter using massive piles of discarded shells for pavement and mortar.

NYC DOT has explored melting plastic waste into hot asphalt to increase road durability and decrease potholes.

Some might say this city is trash, but they’re missing the gems.

04 / Broome & Chrystie

125 Chrystie’s
first USPS delivery:
I WAS THERE

By Helena Wentworth

Longtime residents know that the block of Broome between Bowery and Chrystie has been suffering constant construction since at least 2021. The northwest corner of Broome and Chrystie (125 Chrystie) was previously an empty warehouse with a rotating cast of uninspiring murals—the backdrop of countless tourist photoshoots and at least one amateur music video.

The new building got its first USPS delivery on August 25th, and its first residents on September 1st.
